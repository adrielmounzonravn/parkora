# Parkora evidence: survey and competitor research

**Date:** 2026-10-14 · Appendix to [plan.md](plan.md).

The survey was run in Cochabamba, Bolivia, the planned MVP pilot city; the product itself targets cities in Latin America and beyond. The survey was run in Spanish. Questions, answer options, and respondent quotes below are translated from Spanish.

## A. Survey questions and scoring

**Format:** Google Forms (or similar) · **Length:** ~4 min · **Target:** 30+ responses
**Distribution:** WhatsApp groups, WhatsApp statuses, acquaintances who drive.

> **Design rule:** sections 1 to 4 ask about **facts and real behavior** and don't mention the app. The idea only appears in section 5, so as not to bias the earlier answers.

### Form introduction text

> **What is parking in Cochabamba like?**
> We're running a short study about the experience of driving and parking in the city. It takes about 4 minutes and it's anonymous. We're not selling anything. Thank you!

### Section 1: Profile

**1. How often do you drive in Cochabamba?** *(single choice, required)*
- Every day
- 3 to 5 days a week
- 1 to 2 days a week
- Less than once a week
- I don't drive → *send to the end of the form*

### Section 2: Areas and the problem

**2. In which areas of Cochabamba is it hard for you to find parking?** *(checkboxes, multiple choice)*
- Centro / Casco Viejo
- La Cancha
- El Prado / Av. Ballivián
- Recoleta / Av. Pando / Av. América
- Hospital and clinic area
- University area
- Queru Queru / Cala Cala
- None in particular
- Other: ______

**3. How often do you drive to any of those areas?** *(single choice)*
- Almost every day
- Several times a week
- Once a week
- A few times a month
- Almost never

**4. The last time you went, how long did it take you to find parking?** *(single choice)*
- Less than 5 minutes
- 5 to 10 minutes
- 10 to 20 minutes
- More than 20 minutes
- I didn't find any and left or changed plans

**5. When you know parking will be hard, what do you usually do?** *(checkboxes, multiple choice)*
- I circle around until I find a spot
- I leave earlier
- I park far away and walk
- I pay for a private parking lot
- I take a taxi, *trufi* (shared minibus), or *micro* (bus)
- I leave the car with a car watcher
- I avoid going or go somewhere else
- Other: ______

### Section 3: How you plan

**6. The last time you went to one of those areas, when did you decide you were going?** *(single choice)*
- Days in advance
- The day before
- The same day, a few hours in advance
- Less than 1 hour before

**7. When you head there, do you know what time you'll leave?** *(single choice)*
- Yes, I know the exact time
- Roughly (within about 30 min)
- No, I play it by ear

**8. How long do you usually stay parked in those areas?** *(single choice)*
- Less than 30 minutes
- 30 minutes to 1 hour
- 1 to 2 hours
- 2 to 4 hours
- More than 4 hours

### Section 4: What you pay today

**9. The last time, how much did you pay to leave your car?** *(single choice)*
- Nothing
- Up to Bs 2 (tip to the car watcher)
- Bs 3 to 5
- Bs 6 to 10
- More than Bs 10

### Section 5: An idea

> *Section text:* Imagine an app where you see **in real time** which on-street spots are free in the area you're going to, and you can **reserve one** from the time you arrive until the time you leave. You pay through the app and the spot waits for you.

**10. How useful would it be for you?** *(linear scale 1–5)*
1 = Not useful at all · 5 = Very useful

**11. Compared with what you pay today, how much would you pay to have a guaranteed spot?** *(single choice)*
- I wouldn't pay for this
- Less than I pay today
- The same as I pay today
- A little more
- Quite a bit more, if it really guarantees me the spot

**12. What would worry you or make you not use it?** *(paragraph, optional)*

**13. Would you like to try it when it's ready? Leave us your WhatsApp.** *(short answer, optional)*

### How to read the results (set before receiving responses)

| Criterion | Questions | Counts as "yes" |
|---|---|---|
| 1. ≥60% go ≥1 time/week to an area where they lose ≥10 min | 3 + 4 | Q3 = "once a week" or more **and** Q4 = "10 to 20 min", "more than 20 min", or "I didn't find any" |
| 2. ≥50% know arrival and departure ≥30 min in advance | 6 + 7 | Q6 ≠ "less than 1 hour before" **and** Q7 = "yes, exact" or "roughly" |
| 3. ≥40% would pay the same or more | 11 (+ 9 as reference) | Q11 = "the same", "a little more", or "quite a bit more" |
| Area rule: whole city or some areas? | 2 | Do 2 or 3 areas add up to ≥50% of mentions? |
| Commitment signal (extra) | 13 | % who leave their WhatsApp |
| Average stay (assumption #8) | 8 | Distribution of answers |

**What happens if a criterion fails (set beforehand):**

| # | If it fails |
|---|---|
| 1 | The problem isn't frequent enough. Drop the idea or look for a narrower niche (e.g., hospitals only). |
| 2 | Since availability is visible in real time, a short lead time is enough (reserving when leaving home). If even that fails, pivot from "reserve" to "see real-time availability" with no reservation. |
| 3 | Revisit the model: keep today's rate, or pivot to private parking lots. |

**Area decision rule (not pass/fail):** if ≥50% of mentions concentrate in 2–3 areas → the pilot starts only in those areas. If mentions are spread across the city → consider a city-wide rollout from the start.

## B. Survey results

**Responses:** 7 (target: 30+) · **Channel:** Google Forms via WhatsApp

> ⚠️ **Small and probably biased sample.** The 7 responses arrived in ~30 minutes, which suggests they come from my close circle. The results are **preliminary**: they serve as a signal, not proof.
>
> **These 7 responses are the final reference sample for the presentation.** No more responses are expected before the deadline.

### Summary

- **2 of 3 criteria pass:** drivers can reserve (86%) and say they would pay the same or more (86%).
- **1 criterion fails:** only 43% lose ≥10 min looking for parking. For most it's 5 to 10 min.
- **Stated interest is very high:** 6 of 7 rated the idea 5/5 for usefulness.
- **Real commitment is not yet proven:** 0 of 7 left their WhatsApp to try it.
- **Next step:** move from what they say to what they do, with a pre-registration test.

### B1. Success / failure criteria

| # | Criterion | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ≥60% go ≥1 time/week to an area where they lose ≥10 min | **3/7 (43%)** | ❌ Fails |
| 2 | ≥50% know arrival and departure ≥30 min in advance | **6/7 (86%)** | ✅ Passes |
| 3 | ≥40% would pay the same or more | **6/7 (86%)** | ✅ Passes |
| — | Commitment signal: leave their WhatsApp | **0/7 (0%)** | ⚠️ Warning |

**Criterion 1 (real and frequent pain):**
- All 7 go to those areas ≥1 time per week (3 almost every day, 3 several times a week, 1 once a week).
- Time spent looking for parking the last time: 2 "I didn't find any and left", 1 "10 to 20 min", **4 "5 to 10 min"**.
- Instrument limitation: the "5 to 10 min" option touches the 10-min threshold exactly, so it doesn't distinguish whether those 4 cases pass or not. The criterion is reported as **not met**, as it was defined.
- **What happens next:** the pre-set action was "drop the idea or look for a narrower niche". The chosen path is the narrowing one: re-test with a corrected survey (B6), and if it fails again, **narrow to the most critical areas or times**. Reasons for not dropping it now are in [plan.md, section 3](plan.md#criterion-1-failed).

**Criterion 2 (they can reserve):**
- When they decided to go: 1 days in advance, 2 the day before, 3 the same day a few hours in advance, 1 less than 1 hour before.
- Departure time: 2 know it exactly, 4 roughly (±30 min), 1 "play it by ear".
- The only one who doesn't meet it is the same respondent in both questions (taxi driver).

**Criterion 3 (willingness to pay):**
- 2 "quite a bit more, if it really guarantees me the spot", 3 "a little more", 1 "the same", 1 "wouldn't pay".
- Perceived usefulness: 6 respondents gave 5/5 and 1 gave 1/5.

### B2. Areas (decision rule)

| Area | Mentions | % of 24 |
|---|---|---|
| Centro / Casco Viejo | 7 | 29% |
| La Cancha | 6 | 25% |
| University area | 6 | 25% |
| Hospital and clinic area | 5 | 21% |
| Prado, Recoleta, Queru Queru, other | 0 | 0% |

- Centro + La Cancha = **54%** → under the rule, this calls for a **pilot focused on Centro and La Cancha**.
- The concentration is weak: almost everyone checked 3 or 4 areas.
- **MVP scope decision:** Centro only (2 or 3 blocks), the most-mentioned area. La Cancha, the university area, and the hospital area show the same problem and are expansion candidates.

### B3. Other findings

**Stay length (assumption #8):**

| Duration | Responses |
|---|---|
| Less than 30 min | 1 |
| 1 to 2 hours | 4 |
| 2 to 4 hours | 2 |

→ More realistic: **~4 to 6 reservations per spot per day**, not 10. The original revenue estimate (10 reservations per spot per day) is inflated ~2x on this side.

**What they pay today:**

| Amount | Responses |
|---|---|
| Nothing | 1 |
| Bs 3 to 5 | 2 |
| Bs 6 to 10 | 4 |

→ Most pay **Bs 6 to 10 (~$0.85 to $1.45)** per visit. With 1-to-4-hour stays at the official Bs 2/h, this is consistent with the official rate (plus *cuidacoches* or a private lot in some cases; see section C1).

**What they do today:** circle around (6/7), leave earlier (5/7), park far away and walk (5/7), pay for a private lot (3/7), taxi or public transport (1/7), avoid going (1/7).

**Concerns (question 12, 6 responses, translated from Spanish):**
- **That the reservation won't be respected** (4/6): "that nobody watches over my reserved spot", "that someone takes my spot", "that I arrive and it isn't free", "that my reservation isn't honored".
- That there won't be spots where I need them (1/6).
- Doesn't apply to their use: taxi driver with short stops (1/6).
- Product idea: **recurring reservation** of the same spot.

### B4. Conclusions

1. **The problem exists and is frequent, but the time lost is less than assumed.** For most it's 5 to 10 minutes; for a minority it's serious (they don't find a spot).
2. **Reserving is compatible with how drivers plan** (assumption #2 preliminarily validated).
3. **Stated interest is high and consistent.** 6 of 7 rated usefulness 5/5, and the same 6 would pay the same or more. The only one not interested (taxi driver) is outside the target segment. Even though the time lost is below the threshold, the idea appeals to them. This suggests that the perceived value isn't just saving minutes, but also **the certainty of having a spot**: "quite a bit more, if it really guarantees me the spot".
4. **That interest is still stated, not demonstrated.** Nobody left their WhatsApp to try it, and the sample probably comes from my close circle, which tends to respond with enthusiasm. There's a gap between what they say and what they do, and closing it is the next experiment.
5. **Sharper customer:** a private driver who stays 1 to 4 hours in Centro or La Cancha. Taxi drivers and short stops are out (casual zones are for them).
6. **The risk that worries drivers most is whether the reservation is honored**, which raises the priority of assumptions #5 and #6 (drivers respecting their windows and enforcement by parking attendants).
7. **The revenue model must be recalculated** with real stay lengths (~4 to 6 reservations per spot per day) and the official rate. Done in [plan.md, section 5](plan.md#5-pricing-marketing-and-first-paying-customers).

### B5. Still not validated

- That the pain is strong enough outside my close circle (sample of 7, probably biased).
- That people actually pay, not just say they would.
- The entire government side (assumption #1), parking attendants (#6), and *cuidacoches* (#7).

### B6. Suggested next experiment

- **Commitment test:** landing page "Reserve your spot in Centro Cochabamba" with pre-registration or pre-reservation. Success: ≥10% of visitors leave their details.
- **Fix the survey:** use ranges that don't touch the threshold (e.g., "less than 10 min" / "10 to 20 min") and distribute it outside my close circle. If criterion 1 still fails (<60%) → narrow to the most critical areas or times.

## C. Competitor research

> ⚠️ **Correction:** an earlier version of the working notes said "no on-street parking apps were identified in Bolivia". **That is not true.** Cochabamba and La Paz already have official apps to **pay** for metered on-street parking (*parqueo tarifado*). What we did **not** find (in Bolivia or, as far as was found, anywhere) is **reserving an on-street spot in advance for private drivers** (only loading zones for delivery drivers, see C2).

### Summary

- **There are two kinds of competitors:** those competing for the **driver** (how they solve the problem today) and those competing for the **contract with the city government** (who supplies the software).
- **For the driver**, the real competition is still the status quo: circling (6/7), leaving earlier (5/7), parking far away (5/7), private lots (3/7).
- **For the city government**, the strongest competitor is **the Cochabamba city government itself**: it already has its app (Innova) and other payment channels. It could add reservations on its own.
- **We found no on-street reservation for private drivers.** Municipal apps only charge for usage. Reservation apps (SpotHero, ParkMobile, Estacionate, YaLlegOK) only reserve garages and private lots. The only on-street reservations found are loading zones for delivery drivers.
- **New risk:** La Paz's metered parking run by private companies was **suspended by the courts** in 2025. This reinforces that assumption #1 (that the government implements it) is the riskiest.

### C1. Direct competitors in Bolivia (on-street parking payment)

#### Cochabamba: the pilot market

| Channel | Operated by | What it does | What it doesn't do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Innova Cochabamba** (app) | Autonomous Municipal Government of Cochabamba | Pay for metered parking by QR: street + plate + time, in 4 steps. Shows metered and no-parking zones. | Doesn't reserve. Doesn't show free spots. |
| **Mis Cuentas** (app and web) | City government (since July 2025) | Pay for metered parking: zone + plate + time. | Same as Innova. |
| **WhatsApp "Luka"**, Multired payment points, parking meter at Plazuela Colón | City government | Other payment channels. | Same. |

**Useful data:**
- **Official rate:** Bs 1 per half hour, Bs 2 per hour (Municipal Decree No. 408).
- **Digital adoption:** in March 2025 only **20%** of drivers paid with Innova. The rest still pay cash to the parking attendants.
- **Charging hours:** 8:00 to 16:00 continuous (since March 2025; verify whether still in force).
- The survey says most pay **Bs 6 to 10** per visit. With 1-to-4-hour stays at Bs 2/h, this is consistent with the official rate (plus *cuidacoches* or a private lot in some cases).

**What it means for us:**
- The city government **already collects digitally**, so the payment infrastructure (QR) and the idea of "paying for parking by app" are not new to the city. That **lowers the adoption barrier**.
- But it also means the city government **already has its own team and app**. The pitch can't be "digitize payment"; it has to be **the reservation** and what it brings (order, less circling, more revenue per spot).
- Realistic option: a reservations module **integrable with Innova / Mis Cuentas in the future** (out of MVP scope) instead of competing with a separate app.

#### La Paz

| Channel | What happened |
|---|---|
| **"Parqueo para Todos"** | Launched in 2025 with 4 private companies and ~6,150 spots. Payment through Mis Cuentas. Differentiated rate: Bs 3 / half hour for La Paz cars, Bs 5 for cars from El Alto and other municipalities. |
| **Suspension** | In November 2025 a Constitutional Chamber upheld the suspension of the service. The new administration announced that parking will be managed directly by the city government ("Calles expeditas"). |

**Lesson:** an on-street parking system run with private operators can fall apart for **legal and political** reasons, not technical ones. That case needs to be understood before going to the Cochabamba city government.

#### Others in Bolivia

- **IA PARK:** shows public parking lots in La Paz and El Alto. Doesn't reserve. Doesn't operate in Cochabamba.
- **parqueo.com.bo:** web directory of parking lots in La Paz.
- **Private parking reservations:** no app was found that reserves private garages in Bolivia.

| **YaLlegOK** (Neuquén, Argentina; launched 2026) | Web app to reserve and pay in advance for private parking lots in central Neuquén. #### Curb reservations for logistics (the closest thing)

| Company | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Coord** (pilot in Aspen, 2021) | Delivery drivers reserved specific loading zones. No longer appears to be active. |
| **Parkunload** (Spain) | Regulates loading zones and limited-time parking with an app, in real time. |
| **Automotus** (Pittsburgh, others) | Smart loading zones with cameras and data. |

**Conclusion:** on-street spot reservations **only exist for delivery drivers** (loading zones), not for private drivers. The industry treats reservations as something for private lots or garages.

**Two readings:**
- 👍 **There's open space:** nobody offers this.
- 👎 **Possible warning sign:** if large companies like ParkMobile, with contracts in hundreds of cities, don't do it, there may be a reason (hard to enforce without sensors, cities don't want to "privatize" the street, low turnover). Worth asking about when talking to the city government.

### C3. The status quo: the driver's real competition

| Alternative | Use it (survey, n=7) | Cost to the driver | Why they would choose Parkora |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circling | 6/7 | 5 to 20 min per visit, stress | They arrive knowing where to park |
| Leaving earlier | 5/7 | Time lost before the appointment | No more need for a time "cushion" |
| Parking far away and walking | 5/7 | Walking, feeling unsafe | Spot close to the destination |
| Private lot | 3/7 | More expensive than the street | Street price (Bs 2/h in Cochabamba) with the same certainty |
| Taxi / public transport | 1/7 | Fare, less convenience | They can keep using their car |
| *Cuidacoches* | — | Tip | Not competing: they keep watching the car, they don't guarantee a spot |

### C4. Why someone would choose Parkora

**Driver:**
- vs. **circling / leaving early:** removes the uncertainty. They leave home knowing where they will park. The survey suggests the value is more in **the certainty** than in the minutes saved ("quite a bit more, if it really guarantees me the spot").
- vs. **parking far away:** a spot close to the destination.
- vs. **a private lot:** the same certainty, at street prices.
- vs. **municipal payment apps (e.g., Innova / Mis Cuentas):** those apps charge once you've **already found** a spot. Parkora **guarantees** the spot before you leave home.

**City government:**
- vs. **digital payment only (what many cities already have):** the **prepaid** reservation secures the payment, provides occupancy data by street and hour, and reduces circling and congestion.
- vs. **handing parking to private operators (the La Paz case):** the city keeps control of rates, zones, and fines, which lowers the political and legal risk. Integration with the city's existing app is possible in the future (out of MVP scope).

### C5. Risks and open questions

1. **The city government can do it alone.** It already has Innova and Mis Cuentas. What stops it from copying the feature? → Possible answer: offer it as an integrated module, not a rival app.
2. **Legal and political risk** (La Paz case). What regulation allows reserving public space? Is a municipal ordinance needed?
3. **Very low rate (in Cochabamba):** at Bs 2/h, a 2-hour reservation brings in Bs 4. At a 10% commission that's **Bs 0.40 per reservation**, and QR payment fees can eat a good part of it. The estimate was recalculated with the official rate in [plan.md, section 5](plan.md#5-pricing-marketing-and-first-paying-customers); rates vary a lot by city and country.
4. **Low digital adoption:** only 20% pay by app today. How many would reserve?
5. **Why nobody else does it.** Find out whether there have been failed attempts at on-street reservations.
